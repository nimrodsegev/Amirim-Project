\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a model's internal state, partway through a familiar phrase,
already holds enough to recover the rest. For fixed expressions in natural text
it often does, and at a useful range.

The same phrases
shown with no surrounding sentence are recovered far less often, so a model
meeting these expressions in real text is in a much better position than an
isolated test would suggest. We further found that outcomes cluster by phrase: a phrase tends to be
recovered in nearly all of its contexts or in almost none, though we do not
separate phrase identity from related properties such as frequency or
tokenization. Additionally, a small probe trained on a single hidden state predicts whether
the model will complete the phrase, at $84.6\%$ precision.

Future work could test the main practical implication, efficiency. Because this
information is available from mid-network states and is consistent within a
phrase, a decoding scheme can potentially use it to commit
several tokens at once rather than paying full depth for each token. We are
pursuing that separately, and an early pilot reproducing CLP
\citep{xie-zhou-2026-clp} on a different model is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}. %; it is separate from the results reported here.
