\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a language model's internal state, partway through a familiar
multi-word expression, already carries enough information to recover the rest.
For fixed expressions in natural text it often does, and at a useful range:
OLMo-2-7B produces the remaining tokens three early in roughly two thirds of
instances and five early in more than half. Recovery clusters strongly by phrase: across a
phrase's natural contexts, success sits at the extremes far more often than an
independent-context model at a common rate would produce. It is also legible from the representation itself, well enough that a small probe reading a single hidden
vector predicts whether the phrase will be completed, at $87.6\%$ precision.

The second finding is a caution about how the first was obtained. Patchscopes
and ordinary generation, run on identical instances, agree on only $78.7\%$ of
them, reverse the ranking of the phrase categories, and, used as probe labels,
locate the relevant information in different parts of the network. Part of that
gap is a budget we chose, since widening the sweep from eight layers to all $32$
recovers six to eight points, but the disagreement itself survives the widest
sweep we can run. Neither instrument is wrong; neither is a neutral window onto
what the model knows. Claims of the form \emph{the model already knows $X$}
should carry the readout that produced them, and the budget it was given.

The practical thread we have not pulled is efficiency. A signal that is
consistent within a phrase, available from mid-network states, and precise
enough to be worth testing as a control signal is the kind of signal a decoding
scheme could use to commit
several tokens at once rather than paying full depth for each. We are pursuing
that separately, and an early pilot reproducing CLP \citep{xie-zhou-2026-clp} on
a different model is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:details}; it does not bear on
anything claimed here. What this paper settles is narrower than the efficiency
question and prior to it: for familiar fixed expressions in English, under two
named readouts, the continuation is often recoverable well before it is read.
