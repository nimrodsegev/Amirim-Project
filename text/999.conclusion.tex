\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a model's internal state, partway through a familiar phrase,
already holds enough to recover the rest. For fixed expressions in natural text
it often does, and at a useful range: reading a single hidden state with
Patchscopes recovers the remaining tokens in $57.5\%$ of instances three tokens
before the phrase ends, and in $38.7\%$ five tokens before. The same phrases
shown with no surrounding sentence are recovered far less often, so a model
meeting these expressions in real text is in a much better position than an
isolated test would suggest. Results group tightly by phrase: a phrase tends to
be recovered in nearly all of its contexts or in almost none, though we do not
separate phrase identity from related properties such as frequency or
tokenization. And the signal is legible from the state itself,
well enough that a small probe reading a single hidden vector predicts whether
the model will complete the phrase, at $87.6\%$ precision.

Patchscopes recovery depends on the layer budget: widening the sweep from eight
layers to all $32$ raises agreement with generation from $78.7\%$ to $81.1\%$.
Patchscopes rates are worth reporting together with the layers that produced
them.

We have not tested the main practical implication, efficiency. A signal that is
consistent within a phrase, available from mid-network states, and precise
enough to be worth testing as a control signal is the kind of signal a decoding
scheme could use to commit several tokens at once rather than paying full depth
for each. We are pursuing that separately, and an early pilot reproducing CLP
\citep{xie-zhou-2026-clp} on a different model is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}; it does not bear on anything claimed here. What this
paper shows is narrower: for familiar fixed expressions in English, the
continuation is often recoverable well before it is read.
