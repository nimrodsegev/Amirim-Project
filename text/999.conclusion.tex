\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a model's internal state, partway through a familiar phrase,
already holds enough to recover the rest. For fixed expressions in natural text
it often does, and at a useful range: OLMo-2-7B recovers the remaining tokens
at $i=3$ in roughly two thirds of instances and at $i=5$ in more than half. Recovery clusters strongly by phrase:
across a phrase's natural contexts, success sits at the extremes far more than an
independent-context model at a common rate would produce. It is also legible from the representation itself, well enough that a small probe reading a single hidden
vector predicts whether the phrase will be completed, at $87.6\%$ precision.

The second finding is a caution about how the first was obtained. The two
readouts agree on most instances, but where they part they part in one
direction: generation recovers continuations Patchscopes misses about three
times as often as the reverse. A Patchscopes rate is therefore better read as a
floor than as an estimate, and part of even that gap is a choice rather than a
finding, since widening the sweep from eight layers to all $32$ recovers six to
eight points. What does not wash out is where the signal appears to live. Train
the same probe on one readout's labels and it peaks in the middle of the
network; train it on the other's and it peaks at the last layer. Neither
instrument is wrong; neither is a neutral window onto what the model knows.
Claims of the form \emph{the model already knows $X$} should carry the readout
that produced them, and the budget it was given.

The practical thread we have not pulled is efficiency. A signal that is
consistent within a phrase, available from mid-network states, and precise
enough to be worth testing as a control signal is the kind of signal a decoding
scheme could use to commit
several tokens at once rather than paying full depth for each. We are pursuing
that separately, and an early pilot reproducing CLP \citep{xie-zhou-2026-clp} on
a different model is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:details}; it does not bear on
anything claimed here. What this paper settles is narrower than the efficiency
question and prior to it: for familiar fixed expressions in English, under two
named readouts, the continuation is often recoverable well before it is read.
