\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a model's internal state, partway through a familiar phrase,
already holds enough to recover the rest. For fixed expressions in natural text
it often does, and at a useful range. Reading a single hidden state with
Patchscopes recovers the remaining tokens in $56.6\%$ of cases three tokens
before the phrase ends, and in $37.3\%$ five tokens before. The same phrases
shown with no surrounding sentence are recovered far less often, so a model
meeting these expressions in real text is in a much better position than an
isolated test would suggest. Outcomes cluster by phrase. A phrase tends to be
recovered in nearly all of its contexts or in almost none, though we do not
separate phrase identity from related properties such as frequency or
tokenization. A small probe trained on a single hidden state predicts whether
the model will complete the phrase, at $84.6\%$ precision.

Patchscopes recovery depends on the layer budget. Widening the sweep from eight
layers to all $32$ raises agreement with generation from $77.8\%$ to $80.4\%$.
Patchscopes results should therefore specify which layers were included.

We have not tested the main practical implication, efficiency. Because this
information is available from mid-network states and is consistent within a
phrase, future work could test whether a decoding scheme can use it to commit
several tokens at once rather than paying full depth for each token. We are
pursuing that separately, and an early pilot reproducing CLP
\citep{xie-zhou-2026-clp} on a different model is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}; it is separate from the results reported here. Our
result is narrower than that question. For familiar fixed expressions in
English, the continuation is often recoverable well before it is read.
