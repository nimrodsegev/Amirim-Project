\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\input{tables/dataset}

We describe the phrases we test, the sentences we test them in, and the models
we run them on.

\subsection{Phrases}

We assemble $1{,}164$ fixed multi-word expressions of three kinds
(Table~\ref{tab:dataset}): \textbf{idioms} ($852$), fixed by convention;
\textbf{landmarks} ($127$), fixed by reference; and \textbf{film titles}
($185$), fixed by citation. All three should be frequent in pretraining text,
which is what makes them a favourable test case. The idioms come from the four
sources used to build IDIOMEM \citep{haviv-etal-2023-understanding}: MAGPIE
\citep{haagsma-etal-2020-magpie} ($511$),
LIdioms \citep{moussallem-etal-2018-lidioms} ($165$),
EPIE \citep{saxena-paul-2020-epie} ($81$), and a public list of English idioms
and proverbs ($95$).\footnote{\url{https://www.ef.com/wwen/english-resources/english-idioms/},
the source \citet{haviv-etal-2023-understanding} label \texttt{EF}. Our set is
not IDIOMEM itself: we keep items their predictability and prompt-similarity
filters remove, since our success criterion is a multi-token string match rather
than a single-token prediction.} Landmarks are the multi-word entries of a public
``150 most famous landmarks in the world'' list and film titles the multi-word
entries of the IMDb Top 250; neither is a curated research resource. Phrases too
short to admit a lookahead position of at least $2$ are excluded.

\subsection{Natural contexts}

We also collect real occurrences, since a phrase shown on its own gives the
model no sign that a set phrase is what it is reading. Streaming $6.3$ million
documents from FineWeb-Edu \citep{penedo-etal-2024-fineweb}, we keep up to ten
sentences per phrase, each with up to $150$ tokens of preceding
text.\footnote{For phrases that are predominantly capitalised (most landmarks
and titles), we require the occurrence to preserve at least $70\%$ of the
original capitalisation, which prevents a sentence about sunrise from being
collected as an instance of the film \textit{Before Sunrise}.} This gives two
views of the same expression: \textit{Empire \underline{\hspace{1.2em}}} on its
own, and \textit{he went to NYC to see the Empire \underline{\hspace{1.2em}}}.

This yields $9{,}955$ (phrase, context) instances covering $1{,}096$ of the
$1{,}164$ phrases. $44.8\%$ of the instances are duplicates, leaving $5{,}498$
distinct pairs. Decoding is greedy, so a duplicate gives the same outcome and
overall rates barely move ($64.8\%$ versus $64.5\%$ at $i=3$), but the effective
sample is about half the instance count; we therefore compute every per-phrase
statistic (\S\ref{sec:stability}) over distinct contexts only. Each instance
gives one trial per usable lookahead distance, $24{,}740$ in all at
$i \in \{2,3,4,5\}$. Idioms dominate these trials ($20{,}371$ of them), so
aggregate rates run close to idiom rates, and we report per-category results
separately in \S\ref{sec:disagreement}.

\subsection{Models}

Our main model is OLMo-2-7B \citep{olmo-2025-olmo2} in \texttt{bfloat16}, a
$32$-layer decoder-only model whose training data is public, which makes the
assumption that these phrases were seen in pretraining checkable in principle.
We replicate the central measurements on OLMo-3-7B and, for generation, on
Qwen2.5-14B \citep{qwen-2024-qwen25} (Appendix~\ref{app:details}). All three are
base checkpoints.

Our primary Patchscopes measurement takes the union over eight layers,
$\{5, 7, 10, 13, 15, 20, 25, 30\}$: the layer set on which the per-layer,
per-category and probing analyses were run, since storing hidden states for all
$32$ layers at every trial was not feasible. Where a wider sweep is available we
report it alongside, and \S\ref{sec:disagreement} quantifies what the budget
costs.
