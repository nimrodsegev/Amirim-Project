\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Phrases are recovered several tokens ahead}
\label{sec:lookahead}

\input{tables/main_results}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/context_vs_isolation}
\caption{Recovery rate against lookahead distance, for both readouts, with and
without natural context. Both Patchscopes curves sweep all $32$ layers, so the
context comparison is like for like; the eight-layer budget used elsewhere in
the paper appears in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Presented alone the two readouts are
nearly indistinguishable. In context both rise sharply, by $25$--$36$ points for
generation and $22$--$33$ for Patchscopes, and generation pulls slightly ahead.}
\label{fig:context}
\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the central measurement. In natural context, the
remaining tokens of a familiar phrase appear in OLMo-2-7B's next $i+3$ generated
tokens in $84.0\%$ of instances at $i=2$, $64.8\%$ at $i=3$ and $53.6\%$ at
$i=5$. Anticipation
of fixed expressions is therefore not a marginal effect at short range; it
persists at distances of several tokens, well past the horizon of ordinary
next-token prediction.

\paragraph{Isolation understates the effect.}
The same phrases presented alone, with no surrounding text, are recovered far
less often: $59.2\%$ at $i=2$ falling to $17.6\%$ at $i=5$ (Figure~\ref{fig:context}).
The generation gap widens with distance, from $24.8$ points at $i=2$ to $36.0$
at $i=5$. Patchscopes benefits substantially too, by $21.8$ to $33.2$ points,
though not in a steadily widening way. This is the expected
direction, since context supplies both topical evidence and the signal that a
fixed expression is under way, but its size matters for interpreting prior
results. A bare phrase is a poor proxy for the condition
under which a model actually encounters these expressions: for these phrases
and models, measuring in isolation substantially underestimates recovery in
natural context.

\paragraph{In isolation the two readouts nearly coincide.}
The lower pair of curves in Figure~\ref{fig:context} is the isolated condition,
and the two readouts track each other within a point or two at every distance:
$57.8\%$ against $59.2\%$ at $i=2$, $16.5\%$ against $17.6\%$ at $i=5$. Whatever
separates them in context (\S\ref{sec:disagreement}) does not appear when the
phrase stands alone, itself a clue that the disagreement is something context
creates rather than a fixed property of the instruments.

\paragraph{Successes persist beyond the range we analyse.}
We cap the analysis at $i=5$ because sample sizes fall steeply beyond it: $185$
phrases at $i=6$ and $51$ at $i=8$ in the isolated condition. The effect does
not stop there: in context, generation recovers $48.9\%$ of the $1{,}123$ trials
at $i=6$ and $41.7\%$ of the $312$ at $i=8$. We draw no conclusions from so
sparse a tail. We report it only because an earlier version
of this work found zero successes at $i \geq 6$, which was an artifact of a
fixed generation budget (\S\ref{sec:criterion}) rather than a property of the
model.

\subsection{Recovery varies systematically by phrase}
\label{sec:stability}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/per_phrase_consistency}
\caption{Share of its own distinct contexts in which each phrase's continuation
is recovered at $i=3$, generation readout, over the $803$ phrases with at least
five distinct contexts. Grey is the binomial distribution expected if every
context were an independent draw at the pooled rate $p=0.64$. The two extreme
bins are kept separate because most phrases have exactly five distinct contexts.
The observed distribution puts mass at both ends, where the null has almost
none: $7.7\%$ of phrases fail everywhere and $31.5\%$ succeed everywhere,
against $0.5\%$ and $10.1\%$ under the null; the null instead piles up in the middle, where the observed
distribution is thin.}
\label{fig:consistency}
\end{figure}

An aggregate rate of $64.8\%$ at $i=3$ is consistent with two very different
pictures. Either the model recovers most phrases most of the time with some
per-occasion noise, or it reliably recovers some phrases and reliably fails on
others. Only the second supports the idea of a stable, reusable phrase-level
representation.

For each of the $803$ phrases with at least five distinct contexts
(\S\ref{sec:setup}) we compute the share of its contexts that succeed, and
compare the resulting distribution against the binomial null in which every
context is an independent draw at the pooled rate $p = 0.645$
(Figure~\ref{fig:consistency}). The two differ sharply. $31.5\%$ of phrases are
recovered in \emph{every} context, where the null predicts $10.1\%$, and
$7.7\%$ in none at all, where it predicts $0.5\%$. The null instead concentrates
in the middle, putting $56.0\%$ of its mass in the two central bins against
$32.3\%$ observed. The Patchscopes readout is more extreme still, with $37.4\%$
of phrases recovered in at least $90\%$ of their contexts against a null of
$9.5\%$.

What this rules out is the picture in which every context is an independent
draw at a common rate: the between-phrase spread is far larger than that model
allows. Some expressions are recovered across most of their occurrences and
others across almost none. It does not establish that the phrase \emph{itself}
is what varies, since we have not separated phrase identity from its
correlates: pretraining frequency, length, tokenization, category, or how
similar a phrase's contexts happen to be (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). What it does
establish is that outcomes cluster strongly by phrase: contexts carrying the
same expression behave far more alike than a common-rate independent model
predicts. Whether that clustering is caused by phrase identity itself or by
correlated properties we did not measure is open. Either way it is what makes
the probing question in \S\ref{sec:probe} worth asking: a signal that is
consistent within a phrase is one that something in the representation could
carry.

\paragraph{Where in the model it becomes readable.}
Recording Patchscopes success at each probed layer separately gives the same
shape at every distance: recovery is low early, rises through the middle of the
network, peaks at L25 and falls back at L30 (Appendix~\ref{app:details}). The
late decline may be the last layers specialising toward the actual next token
rather than maintaining a transferable representation, consistent with the
decline \citet{kaplan-etal-2025-tokens} report for words, though we do not
establish it. Over all $32$ layers the median first successful layer is L16 and
$41.9\%$ of successes appear at or before L10, so phrase information becomes
readable gradually and, for a large minority, early.

\subsection{The two readouts disagree}
\label{sec:disagreement}

\input{tables/disagreement_examples}
\input{tables/categories}

So far we have reported both readouts as if they were interchangeable
measurements of one underlying quantity. They are not. Table~\ref{tab:agreement}
compares them on identical instances (same phrase, same context, same cut
point, same success criterion), and they agree on $78.7\%$ of the $24{,}740$
pooled trials.

The residual $21.3\%$ is the informative part. It is lopsided: generation
recovers a continuation that Patchscopes misses in $15.7\%$ of instances, while
Patchscopes recovers one that generation misses in only $5.6\%$, roughly a
three-to-one asymmetry. Read on its own, that says Patchscopes undercounts what
the model can do.

The two failure directions look qualitatively different, and neither is simply
an instrument defect. The examples in Table~\ref{tab:disagree} are illustrative
rather than a systematic sample, so what follows is a reading of them, not a
measured property of the disagreement. Where Patchscopes succeeds and
generation does not, the intervention can elicit the phrase from the state
while the surrounding context steers generation elsewhere: given
\textit{Why should one person be brought up}, it writes \textit{a wealthy
family, with every opportunity} rather than \textit{in the lap of luxury}. That
is a different continuation, not a wrong one, and a reminder that our criterion
asks for a specific string rather than a good completion. Where generation
succeeds and Patchscopes does not, the patchscope reaches for a neighbour: for
\textit{a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush} it returns \textit{worth
more than one}. There the readout recovered the sense and lost the wording.

\paragraph{The asymmetry is partly a layer-budget artifact.}
The comparison above gives Patchscopes eight layers. Sweeping all $32$ raises
its in-context rate from $73.2\%$ to $79.6\%$ at $i=2$ and from $38.7\%$ to
$46.4\%$ at $i=5$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}), lifts agreement to $81.1\%$, and
rebalances the disagreement from three-to-one to roughly three-to-two
(Table~\ref{tab:agreement}). At $i=3$ the two readouts then differ by $0.7$
points overall. Most of the apparent superiority of generation is therefore a
statement about how thoroughly Patchscopes was sampled, not about the model.
What survives the wider sweep is the disagreement itself: even at $32$ layers,
$18.9\%$ of instances are classified differently, and $7.8\%$ are recovered only
by Patchscopes. The two instruments are not nested, and no layer budget makes
one a refinement of the other.

We keep the eight-layer readout as our primary measurement because every
per-layer, per-category and probing analysis was run on that layer set. Readers
comparing our Patchscopes rates against other work should note that the budget
moves them by six to eight points.

\paragraph{Category difficulty is readout-dependent.}
Table~\ref{tab:categories} breaks the pooled rates down by phrase kind, and the
two readouts do not merely differ in level; they reverse an ordering. Under
Patchscopes, idioms are the \emph{easiest} category at $57.4\%$, ahead of film
titles ($54.7\%$) and landmarks ($47.2\%$). Under generation, idioms are the
\emph{hardest} at $66.4\%$, well behind film titles ($84.5\%$) and landmarks
($75.3\%$). One explanation, which we have not tested, is the
scoring artifact of \S\ref{sec:falsepos}: idioms have accepted variants, so
exact-match scoring would penalise them most under the readout that produces
the most fluent continuations. Confirming that would need the adjudication
repeated per category. Under the wider $32$-layer sweep idioms move to the middle of the
Patchscopes ranking rather than the top, so the reversal weakens to a
re-ordering, but the categories are still ranked differently by the two
instruments.

The practical consequence is narrow but concrete. A study measuring only with
Patchscopes would report that this model handles idioms better than landmarks or
titles; one measuring only with generation would report the opposite. Both would
be reporting their instrument as much as their model.

\subsection{The hidden state predicts generation success}
\label{sec:probe}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/probe_balanced_accuracy}
\caption{Balanced accuracy of the two probes by layer, against the raw accuracy
a majority-class guess would achieve on the Patchscopes label. That baseline
moves from $93.4\%$ at L5 to $52.3\%$ at L25 as the positive rate shifts, which
is why raw accuracy on that label cannot be compared across layers and balanced
accuracy is used instead. The label then determines the shape: predicting
Patchscopes success peaks in the middle layers, while predicting generation
success trends upward to a maximum at L30.}
\label{fig:probes}
\end{figure}

If phrase anticipation is carried by the representation, it should be readable
from the representation without an intervention. We train a small MLP on the raw
hidden state at the cut position to predict whether the readout succeeds, pooling
$i \in \{2,3,4,5\}$ and splitting by phrase so that no test phrase is seen in
training (Appendix~\ref{app:details}).

Predicting \emph{generation success} works well. Balanced accuracy trends
upward with depth, from $69.8\%$ at L5 to a maximum of $76.8\%$ at L30 with a
small dip at L20, and precision
is high and nearly flat at $83.3\%$ to $87.6\%$ (full metrics in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}). A
positive call from this probe is right roughly seven times in eight, and it is
made from a single hidden vector, before the model has produced any of the
tokens in question.

\paragraph{The signal is learned, not a base rate.}
Because $69.1\%$ of instances are positive, a probe could score respectably by
guessing. Balanced accuracy corrects for that by construction, and running the
same probe on the earliest layers gives a clean gradient: $58.6\%$ at L0, the raw
embedding, rising through $65.1\%$ at L1 and $66.5\%$ at L2 to $69.8\%$ at
L5 (Appendix~\ref{app:details}). A base-rate artifact would be flat; this
climbs as computation accumulates. That L0 is already above chance does suggest
part of the signal is lexical identity of the cut token rather than context.

\paragraph{The label changes where the ``best'' layer is.}
Training the identical architecture on the Patchscopes label instead produces a
different profile: balanced accuracy peaks in the middle of the network
($76.5\%$ at L10, $76.3\%$ at L15) and is worst at L30 ($66.4\%$), the mirror
image of the generation-label curve (Figure~\ref{fig:probes}). Raw accuracy for
this probe is not comparable across layers at all, because the positive rate
moves from $6.6\%$ at L5 to $47.7\%$ at L25 as Patchscopes itself gets better.
A reader interested in \emph{where in the model phrase information lives} would
draw opposite conclusions from the two probes. We read the generation-label
probe as the more trustworthy of the two, since its label does not depend on the
layer being probed, but the divergence is itself the point.

The same mid-network peak appears when we ask whether the Patchscopes-label
probe tracks phrase identity rather than a general notion of difficulty.
Comparing each held-out phrase's mean predicted probability with its true
success rate, the correlation peaks at $r=0.62$ (L10) and falls away at both
ends, to $0.41$ at L5 and $0.48$ at L30 (Appendix~\ref{app:details}). We have
not run the equivalent analysis on the generation-label features, so this says
where \emph{Patchscopes} success becomes phrase-predictable, not where the model's
own behaviour does.

\subsection{Most apparent errors are valid alternates}
\label{sec:falsepos}

The probe's precision is bounded by the scoring criterion, not only by the
model. We adjudicated all $412$ false positives of the generation-label probe at
L25, cases where the probe predicted completion and exact-match scoring
recorded a failure, by presenting each (target, generated text) pair to
ChatGPT with a three-way rubric (Appendix~\ref{app:judge}). It labelled $259$
cases ($62.9\%$) valid alternates, $72$ ($17.5\%$) partially correct and $81$
($19.7\%$) unrelated. Typical cases are \textit{laughs best} for \textit{laughs
loudest} and \textit{beat around the bush} for \textit{beat about the bush}: the
more common form of the expression, scored as a failure.

This is a single judge, with no human validation and no record of which ChatGPT
version produced it, so we report it as indicative of the direction of the bias
rather than as a corrected accuracy. Read that way, roughly four in five of
these apparent errors reflect the rigidity of exact matching rather than a model
that did not know the phrase. These cases were selected by the probe, not
sampled from all exact-match failures, so this demonstrates a limitation of
exact matching within that subset; it does not establish the direction or size
of any bias in the recovery rates of \S\ref{sec:lookahead}. An independent split
of the same cases by the model's own confidence agrees and uses no judge at all: in $40.0\%$ the model was itself confident ($>0.7$), and
in only $10.4\%$ unsure ($<0.3$).

\paragraph{The model's own confidence tracks the observed outcome.}
Taking the geometric mean of the probability the model assigns to each token it
generates, confidence averages $0.878$ on successful completions and $0.447$ on
failures. Split by probe outcome at L25 it follows the true label rather than
the probe's guess: true positives average $0.904$ and false negatives $0.791$,
against $0.599$ for false positives and $0.414$ for true negatives
(Appendix~\ref{app:details}). When probe prediction and generation outcome
disagree, the model's confidence tracks the outcome more closely than the
prediction, on average. Confidence and probe output may therefore be
complementary signals, though we evaluate no combined decision rule.
