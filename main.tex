\begin{abstract}

Language models produce one token per forward pass, but when they write a
familiar multi-word expression, the next few words are largely settled before
they are written. We ask whether this shows up inside the model: partway through
such a phrase, does the hidden state already carry enough information to recover
the words still to come? We test this on OLMo-2-7B with $1.1$k idioms, landmark
names and film titles, stopping the model at a controlled \emph{lookahead
distance} $i$ and reading the hidden state at that point with Patchscopes. Across
$5.5$k naturally occurring phrase-context pairs, the remaining tokens of a
phrase are
recovered in $56.6\%$ of instances three tokens before the phrase ends and
$37.3\%$ five tokens before. The same phrases shown with no surrounding sentence
are recovered far less often, so a model meeting these expressions in real text
is in a much better position than an isolated test would suggest. Interestingly, recovery is highly consistent within a phrase: across natural contexts, a
phrase tends to be recovered almost always or almost never. We further show that a small probe trained on a single hidden state, predicts whether the model will complete the phrase at $84.6\%$ precision. Our results show models carry usable information about words they
have not yet produced, and that information can be detected before those words
appear.
