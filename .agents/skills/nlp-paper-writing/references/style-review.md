# Mandatory style review

Run this pass before presenting any draft, revision, or review, including a local edit. It distills the repository's three style readings—`Global Coherence and Internal Structure`, `Joschua Schimel on Flow`, and `Williams and Bizup on Elegance`—into an operational reader-following check: first establish the sense of the whole, then repair internal structure and flow, and only then add controlled emphasis. Do not change claims, evidence, qualifications, terminology, citations, or unresolved placeholders during this pass unless the requested task includes that change.

## Global coherence

- Can the unit be summarized as a short claim chain? Does each conclusion follow from the premises before it?
- Does the opening state the concrete question, problem, or purpose and announce the themes or stages that follow?
- Are those themes developed in the announced order with stable terminology?
- Does every paragraph contribute to the unit's point? Move or cut material that belongs to another story arc instead of forcing a local transition.
- Does the organization follow the reader's logic—causal, logical, or coordinate—rather than the chronology of discovery?
- Does each paragraph have one dominant job and a deliberate opening, development, and resolution?
- Is the final paragraph or sentence doing more than repeating a slogan? Does it state the supported consequence, qualification, or next step?

## Flow and internal structure

- Does each sentence begin with familiar information that looks backward and launch the next idea? Make the topic the reader already knows and the stress position carry the important new information.
- Can the stress of one sentence become the topic of the next? Link concepts, not just exact words, and preserve exact repetition when it is clearer than a synonym.
- Do paragraph endings resolve their story arc, and do the next paragraph openings pick up that resolution? Mark shifts from motivation to method, method to result, result to explanation, or evidence to limitation.
- Are short topic-topic lists intentional and coherent? If a new arc begins, make the bridge explicit or start a separate paragraph.
- If a transition sentence fixes one boundary but breaks the next, restructure the arc rather than adding more connective language.
- Are first-time readers able to reconstruct why each sentence and subsection appears at that point?

## Per-paragraph post-writing pass

Before finalizing, inspect every paragraph in order; do not treat this as a spot check. For each paragraph:

1. Label its one dominant job and verify that the first sentence makes that job or point visible.
2. Confirm that every sentence supports that job, with claims paired to nearby evidence, explanation, or necessary setup.
3. Trace the topic–stress relay sentence by sentence: familiar information should launch each sentence, important new information should receive emphasis, and the next sentence should pick up the prior stress when possible.
4. Check that the paragraph develops one story arc without an unresolved topic switch, and that its final sentence resolves the point or creates a clear bridge to the next paragraph.
5. Check the paragraph's terminology, qualifications, sentence endings, parallel comparisons, and cadence. Keep an acceptable stylistic choice when changing it would not improve clarity, logic, precision, or voice.

For a local edit, check the edited paragraph plus the paragraphs immediately before and after it; for a global pass, complete the loop for the entire unit.

## Clarity, concision, and restrained elegance

- Are the main characters easy to find in subject position and their actions expressed as verbs when that improves clarity? Keep passive voice when the object, process, or parallel structure should remain central.
- Are subjects close to their verbs, conditions close to the claims they qualify, and pronouns unambiguous?
- Has redundancy been removed without deleting mechanisms, examples, evidence, controls, or qualifications?
- Do important comparisons use parallel structure, and do lists follow a visible order? Use balance as a way to clarify relationships, not as ornament.
- Do important sentences end on the result, contrast, implication, or central concept rather than a weak placeholder?
- Is sentence length varied for the reasoning and cadence? Avoid both uniform clause chains and artificial staccato.
- Does the prose remain understated? Let precision, structure, and consequence create interest; do not add praise, decorative symmetry, or conspicuous rhetorical devices merely to sound elegant.

## Final adversarial check

Read the unit once as a skeptical first-time reader. Identify one place where the argument, transition, or emphasis could be misunderstood. Fix the wording or structure if the evidence supports a fix; otherwise flag the unresolved question rather than guessing.
