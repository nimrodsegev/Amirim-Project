# Conclusion

## Job

Leave the reader with the paper's durable answer: what was established, by what
evidence, within what scope, and why it changes understanding or practice. The
conclusion should synthesize at a higher level than the abstract rather than
repeat it sentence by sentence.

## Shape the ending

Return to the problem and named contribution, then connect the strongest pieces
of evidence into one conclusion. State the implication that follows from those
findings and, when useful, the most important boundary or next question.

The conclusion may be short when the paper has already developed its discussion,
but it must still do conceptual work. A longer Discussion and Conclusion may
compare interpretations or consequences; preserve the distinction between what
the paper demonstrates and what it suggests.

## Keep it earned

- Do not introduce a new result, method component, citation-dependent argument,
  or unexamined application.
- Do not close with generic claims that the work is important, promising, or
  opens many directions. Name the concrete capability, insight, or changed
  assumption.
- Do not let future work replace the paper's present conclusion.
- Keep terminology and confidence aligned with the abstract, results, and
  limitations.
- End on the central concept or consequence rather than an implementation detail
  or vague slogan.

## Source basis

Primarily *Ever Growing Academic Writing*, PDF pages 3 and 25, on consistent
confidence and using conclusion/discussion for deeper implications; Vered
Shwartz's *Tips for Writing NLP Papers*, PDF pages 1–3, on the conclusion as
part of the paper's stable narrative and on stating findings without suspense;
and *Academic Writing for NLP*, PDF page 28, on compressing the work into a final
paragraph. The slide's dismissive treatment of conclusions is not adopted; the
active rule gives the section a necessary synthesis role.
