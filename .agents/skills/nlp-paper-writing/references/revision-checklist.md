# Revision checklist

Use this as a compact preflight before presenting revised prose. For deeper treatment, consult `writing-brain/INDEX.md` in the writing-kit repository when it is available.

## Argument and evidence

- Can the section be reduced to an atomic claim chain, and does each conclusion follow from the premises before it?
- Can a first-time reader state the paragraph's purpose after its first sentence?
- Does every claim have evidence nearby, and does the wording match the evidence's scope?
- Are observation, interpretation, hypothesis, and speculation clearly distinguished?
- Are comparisons explicit about what is held constant and what is not?
- Are limitations placed where they affect interpretation rather than hidden later?
- Does each included result change the conclusion, establish scope, explain a mechanism, answer a likely objection, or enable reproduction?
- Has source abundance been mistaken for importance, or project chronology for reader-facing structure?

## Structure and flow

- Does every subsection add intuition, evidence, or a necessary definition, rather than previewing material owned by another section?
- Is prerequisite prior-work motivation placed before the paper's answer, with detailed positioning reserved for Related Work?
- Does Experimental Setup contain only shared settings, leaving experiment-specific controls with the corresponding results and exhaustive detail in the appendix?
- Does each paragraph do one job: setup, method, evidence, interpretation, or transition?
- Does information move from familiar to new?
- Does each transition name the relationship—cause, contrast, consequence, qualification, or next test?
- Does the paragraph end by resolving its point rather than opening an unrelated topic?
- Do section openings explain why the section exists and how it advances the paper?

## Sentence quality

- Is the opening paper-specific, or could it introduce many neighboring papers unchanged?
- Put the main character in subject position and the main action in a verb.
- Keep subjects close to their verbs; move conditions near the claims they qualify.
- Replace vague references such as “this,” “these results,” and “together” when the antecedent is not immediate.
- Remove repeated meaning and stacked qualifications, but preserve motivation, mechanisms, and scientific support.
- Use terminology consistently. Avoid synonym drift for key concepts.
- Reserve “surprisingly,” “significantly,” “robust,” and similar words for cases where they are defined or supported.
- For an important paragraph, were several genuinely different formulations considered before choosing one coherent version?

## Endings

- Does the final introduction or conclusion paragraph synthesize more than one established claim?
- Does it show what the work changes or enables without announcing that the reader should find it important?

## Final adversarial check

Ask: What would a skeptical reviewer misunderstand, dispute, or ask to quantify? Fix unclear wording; do not fabricate the missing evidence.

## Mandatory style review

Before presenting the result, run [style-review.md](style-review.md). At minimum, verify:

- inspect every paragraph in order, labeling its job, checking its evidence and topic–stress relay, and confirming its ending and bridge;
- global themes and paragraph roles are visible and developed in order;
- sentence topics connect familiar information to the previous stress, and paragraph openings pick up prior resolutions;
- structural breaks are repaired by moving, splitting, or cutting material rather than by stacking transition words;
- important contrasts are parallel, important sentences end on the result or implication, and elegance remains subordinate to clarity.
