# Sources and evidence

Use repository sources to ground claims, understand related work, and preserve accurate attribution.

## Source priority

1. The project's raw results and experiment notes for claims about this work.
2. The cited paper itself for claims about prior work.
3. Author-written project notes for intended framing, treated as intent rather than external evidence.
4. Search or secondary summaries only for discovery, followed by verification in a primary source.

## Reading a related paper

Record a compact source note with bibliographic identity or citation key, problem and setting, actual contribution, evaluation scope, relevant limitations, relationship to the current paper, and page or section pointers for claims likely to be cited.

Separate quotation, paraphrase, and the current authors' interpretation. Do not cite a paper for a claim it merely mentions.

## Writing related work

Organize papers by the conceptual axes needed to position this work, not as a chronological list of summaries. Give prior work credit for what it achieves, state the remaining distinction precisely, and avoid straw-man contrasts.

## Results and tables

- Trace every reported number to a raw file, computation, or author-provided value.
- Record units, aggregation, directionality, seeds or samples, and uncertainty when available.
- Distinguish missing values from zero.
- Ensure prose reports the same comparison and metric shown in the table.
- If the provenance or meaning of a value is unclear, stop and ask rather than guessing.

Evidence repositories should be comprehensive; paper narratives should be selective. Use `editorial-taste.md` to decide placement. Selection must never remove unfavorable or null evidence that materially limits a claim.
