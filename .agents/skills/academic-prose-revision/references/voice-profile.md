# Voice profile for technical academic prose

Use this profile as a default only when the author has not supplied a stronger local preference. Author samples and explicit instructions take precedence.

## Target qualities

- **Argument-led:** paragraphs advance a specific claim and make the relation to the preceding claim visible.
- **Paper-specific:** openings begin with the concrete tension, object, or consequence that distinguishes this paper from neighboring work.
- **Technically rich but controlled:** retain mechanisms and useful distinctions; remove implementation inventory that does not help the reader understand or evaluate the claim.
- **Direct without becoming flat:** use clear subjects and verbs, varied sentence lengths, and enough connective tissue for the reasoning to feel continuous.
- **Confident and calibrated:** state supported conclusions directly. Express uncertainty where the evidence is unresolved, not as a habitual hedge.
- **Concrete:** use a representative example, user experience, system scenario, or comparison when it gives the reader a better mental model.
- **Consequential rather than promotional:** make significance visible through what the result enables or changes. Avoid telling readers that a contribution is exciting, important, elegant, or novel.

## Preserve these features

Do not remove a sentence merely because it is long, uses passive voice, contains an adverb, or has a three-part list. Academic prose often needs these forms. Revise only when the construction hides the action, blurs a qualification, creates monotony, or adds no meaning.

Do not flatten a paragraph into short declarative sentences. A strong technical paragraph may need an opening claim, a developed mechanism, a qualification, and a concluding consequence. The goal is controlled movement, not uniform brevity.

## Warning signs

- The first paragraph could introduce many papers in the area without changing its wording.
- Every paragraph uses the same topic-sentence, explanation, contrast, takeaway template.
- Transitions name sequence (“next,” “finally”) without naming the logical relation.
- A contribution is praised more strongly than it is explained.
- A rewrite deletes the example, mechanism, or qualification that made the claim credible.
- The conclusion shrinks to a slogan or repeats the abstract without developing the implication.
- A “de-AI” pass makes the prose telegraphic, emotionally blank, or less recognizably the author's.

## Learning from an author sample

Inspect continuous prose rather than isolated sentences. Note:

1. how quickly the author moves from setting to the paper-specific problem;
2. how much mechanism appears before formalism;
3. typical paragraph length and internal development;
4. preferred ways of expressing contrast, consequence, and qualification;
5. how results are connected to broader implications.

Match these tendencies at the level of reasoning and pacing. Do not import the sample's number of paragraphs, section template, examples, or rhetorical sequence when the new paper needs a different structure.
