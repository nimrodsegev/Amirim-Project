# Academic prose revision procedure

Use this procedure for a full concision, coherence, and flow pass. For a local sentence edit, apply only the relevant checks.

## 1. Preserve the scientific content

Record the claims, evidence, qualifications, technical terms, citations, and unresolved placeholders. A style pass must not silently strengthen a result, remove material counterevidence, or turn a planned experiment into a finding.

## 2. Map the reasoning

Write a short claim map before rewriting:

- What does the reader already accept?
- What new claim does each paragraph add?
- Which evidence or premise supports it?
- What conclusion follows from the claims so far?
- Why does the next paragraph need to exist?

Represent important steps as `A + B -> C` when useful. If `C` does not follow, add the missing premise, narrow the conclusion, or change the order. Do not repair a logical gap with a transition word.

## 3. Test section ownership and global coherence

Ask whether each paragraph performs the job of its current section. Move or cut material that belongs elsewhere:

- Motivation explains why the problem matters and builds intuition; it should not pre-specify the method's interface.
- Method defines the object and mechanism; it should not carry a literature survey.
- Experimental Setup states the common substrate and controls; local settings belong at the start of the corresponding result subsection, with exhaustive configurations in the appendix.
- Results state the comparison and evidence before interpretation.
- Related Work synthesizes cited research and ends each topic with the present paper's precise relation.

Also check the unit's internal architecture: the opening should frame the question and announce the themes, the body should develop them in that order, and the ending should resolve or extend the unit's point. If material belongs to another story arc, move or cut it instead of hiding the break with a transition.

## 4. Generate alternatives

For each important paragraph, consider at least three distinct organizations or phrasings: for example, consequence-first, mechanism-first, or concrete-example-first. Choose based on argumentative fit, precision, pacing, and consistency with the author's sample. Do not combine the strongest sentence from every variant if the result loses a coherent voice.

## 5. Revise at three levels

**Concision:** remove repeated claims, throat-clearing, redundant qualifications, method detail in motivation, and setup detail repeated in results. Keep evidence, causal links, and useful examples.

**Coherence:** ensure every paragraph has one dominant job and that its conclusion follows from its premises. Keep terminology stable and make pronoun antecedents clear.

**Flow:** order sentences from shared context to new information. Use transitions to express cause, contrast, consequence, scope, or continuation. Vary sentence length without manufacturing staccato emphasis.

For the final flow check, use the topic–stress relay: let each sentence begin with familiar information that looks backward and end with the important new information; where possible, make the prior stress the next topic. Link paragraph openings to the preceding paragraph's resolution, especially when changing story arcs. If a local bridge fixes one boundary but breaks the next, restructure the paragraphs rather than adding more transitions.

Before presenting the revision, inspect every paragraph in order. For each one, label its dominant job, verify that every sentence supports it, trace the topic–stress relay, check that the ending resolves or bridges the point, and preserve qualifications, terminology, evidence, and useful rhythm. For a local edit, include the immediately adjacent paragraphs; for a global pass, cover the entire unit.

## 6. Apply restrained elegance

Only after coherence, flow, and concision are sound, use parallel structure for important contrasts, give sentence endings the weight they deserve, and vary sentence length for reasoning and cadence. Keep passive voice, long sentences, repetition, or a rhetorical device when it serves the reader. Do not add ornamental symmetry or praise that the evidence does not support.

## 7. Read adversarially and aloud

Check what a skeptical first-time reader would misunderstand or challenge. Then read for cadence: repeated openings, stacked long sentences, and abrupt paragraph endings often appear only when the prose is heard as a sequence.

## 8. Stop deliberately

Compare the revision with the original. Keep a change only if it improves logic, precision, economy, or voice. A convergence pass should become smaller; if it keeps rewriting whole paragraphs, revisit the claim map instead of continuing stylistic substitution.
